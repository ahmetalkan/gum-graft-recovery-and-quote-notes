Gum Graft Surgery Notes: Preparing for a Periodontal Consult

Gum recession exposes root dentin that lacks the protective enamel shell of a tooth crown, often causing cold sensitivity, root wear, or vulnerability to decay. A gum graft restores protective attached tissue over exposed roots or thickens thin gum margins.

The National Institute of Dental and Craniofacial Research (NIDCR) and the American Dental Association (ADA) note that periodontal disease is a major cause of tissue and bone loss around teeth, while mechanical factors such as aggressive hard-bristle brushing, tooth positioning, and clenching also contribute to localized recession. Controlling the underlying cause matters just as much as the graft itself.

Questions to bring to the periodontist

Background explainers and US periodontist listings are maintained on Gum Graft Cost Guide and its overview of what a gum graft involves.

  • Is my recession progressing, and what underlying cause (inflammation, brushing force, bite, or thin tissue) needs correcting first?
  • Which teeth require grafting now versus simple monitoring?
  • Will tissue come from the roof of my mouth or from processed donor tissue, and why?
  • Will I receive a protective palatal stent to wear while eating during the first week?
Only a periodontal charting exam measuring pocket depth, attached gingiva width, and bone level can confirm whether grafting is indicated for a specific tooth.
